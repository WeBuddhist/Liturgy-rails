oṃ āḥ hūṃ vajra guru padma siddhi hūṃ
I pray to the Dharmakāya Amitābha.
I pray to the Sambhogakāya, the Great Compassionate One.
I pray to the Nirmāṇakāya Padmasambhava.
My Guru is a wondrous emanation body;
Born in the land of India, you engaged in listening and reflection.
Arriving in Central Tibet, you subjugated the arrogant spirits.
Residing in the land of Oḍḍiyāna, you act for the benefit of beings.
When the wonders of your form are seen:
Your right hand makes the mudrā of the sword,
Your left hand makes the mudrā of summoning,
With mouth agape and fangs bared, you gaze upward.
Heir of the Victors, protector of beings. ^1

Through your compassion, grant me your blessings.
Through your love, lead me and others along the path.
Through your enlightened intent, grant me the accomplishments.
Through your power, dispel the obstacles for me and others.
Dispel outer obstacles externally.
Dispel inner obstacles internally.
Dispel secret obstacles into the basic expanse.
With devotion, I prostrate and go for refuge.

When you bound the oath-bound guardians to your command,
In that stainless, supreme site, so delightful to the mind,
On the border between India and Tibet,
You arrived, having bestowed your blessings.
Upon that mountain filled with the fragrance of incense,
Lotus flowers bloom even in winter.
The spring water is the nectar of enlightenment;
In that supreme, blissful sacred place,
Supreme Being of excellent conduct, clad in Dharma robes,
In your right hand, you hold a nine-pointed vajra,
And a precious casket in your left,
Filled within with rakta and nectar.
You bound the ḍākinīs and oath-bound spirits under oath,
Beheld the faces of your meditational deities, and attained accomplishments. ^5

When you established the teachings of the Victorious One,
You performed spiritual practice in the forests of the slate mountains.
Your ritual dagger soared into the expanse of the sky;
Catching it with a vajra mudra, you rolled it between your palms.
Rolling it, you hurled it into the sandalwood forest;
Blazing fires raged and even the oceans were parched.
The tirthikas of the dark slopes and the entire land were incinerated,
And the black yakṣas were ground into dust.

You appeared as a young boy in the attire of a nirmāṇakāya,
With a wondrous, beautiful form and a fine complexion.
Your teeth are perfectly aligned, and your golden hair is beautiful,
In the manner of a youth of sixteen years.
Adorned with various precious jewel ornaments,
In your right hand, you hold a kīla made of bronze,
Suppressing the māras and rākṣasas.
In your left hand, you hold a kīla made of acacia wood,
Granting protection to your devoted children.
Around your neck, you wear a kīla of iron;
You are inseparable from the yidam deity,
Non-dual nirmāṇakāya, Ornament of the World. ^9
